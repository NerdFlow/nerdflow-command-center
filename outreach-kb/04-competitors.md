# 04 — Competitive Landscape

**Purpose:** Background for the ReceptAI sales agent. Use it to understand what a contractor already has (or is weighing) so you can ask good questions and find the gap. Do NOT recite this file to prospects, quote competitor prices as a weapon, or make claims about competitors that aren't in here.

**Product we sell:** ReceptAI by NerdFlow, an AI receptionist for after-hours and overflow calls. It answers, books jobs, and texts the on-call tech. Offer: a free 2-week after-hours pilot, then about $497/month, cancel anytime. Any statement about what ReceptAI can or can't do must match Product Truth. Where this file relies on a capability, it says **[confirm in Product Truth]**.

**Research date:** Prices were checked on **2026-10-05** unless a different date is noted. Prices change often. If a prospect quotes a different number, believe them and don't argue.

**Rules for using this file:**
- "Published" means the vendor's own pricing page showed the number on the check date.
- "Third-party" means the number comes from a review or comparison site, not the vendor. Treat it as approximate.
- "(unverified)" means we could not confirm it from a primary source.
- Many third-party review sites are run by competing AI receptionist vendors, so their "complaints" sections are biased. Weight real review platforms (Trustpilot, Capterra, G2, ConsumerAffairs) more heavily.

## Group A — Human / virtual answering services

These are live-agent call centers. They are the most common thing a 5–50 person contractor already pays for. Their typical model is per-minute or per-call billing on a monthly block, plus overage charges. Most take messages and some will patch through or dispatch to an on-call tech.

### Ruby (ruby.com)
- **Who they target:** General small businesses, with a big base in law firms and professional services. Not trade-specific.
- **Pricing (published, checked 2026-10-05):** Per receptionist minute. Starter is 50 min for $250/mo, Standard is 100 min for $395/mo, Popular is 200 min for $720/mo, and Premium is 500 min for $1,725/mo. Ruby says there are no setup or activation fees and that optional AI enhancements come at no extra cost. Overage rates are not shown on the page. Source: https://www.ruby.com/pricing/
- **Key claims:** US-based, friendly live receptionists, and a "no hidden fees" message.
- **Integrations:** None of ServiceTitan, Housecall Pro, or Jobber showed up on the pricing page. Reviewers ask for better calendar and CRM integration (G2). Check before you claim either way.
- **Common complaints:**
  - Cost.
  - Billing for hang-ups and "dead air" calls. One Capterra reviewer said "15% of my incoming calls were 'Dead Air'."
  - Message quality that varies.
  - Surprise fees at cancellation.
  - Capterra: 3.9/5 from 11 reviews (https://www.capterra.com/p/186839/Ruby-Receptionists-Virtual-Receptionist-and-Chat-Services/reviews/). G2: 3.8/5 from 12 reviews (https://www.g2.com/products/ruby-receptionists/reviews). ConsumerAffairs: 2.0/5 from 29 reviews. Its reviewers mention a 2021 class action over billing (https://www.consumeraffairs.com/business/ruby-receptionists.html). We have not independently verified the lawsuit's details or outcome.

### Smith.ai (smith.ai), human receptionists

- **Pricing (published, checked 2026-10-05):** Per call, not per minute. Starter is 30 calls for $300/mo ($11.50 per extra call), Basic is 90 calls for $810/mo ($10.50 per extra call), and Pro is 300 calls for $2,100/mo ($8.50 per extra call). Billing is month-to-month. Source: https://smith.ai/pricing

- **Common complaints:** Per-call overage adds up fast in busy months, and the service is expensive at contractor call volumes.

### Pattern across Group A
- The usual entry point for real coverage is about **$165–$400/mo for 50–250 minutes**. Overage runs **$1.30–$3.00 per minute** or **$8.50–$11.50 per call**.
- Owners complain far more about **billing surprises (overages, hang-up billing, dead air)** and **message quality** than about the concept itself.
- Most are generalists. They take messages, and only some dispatch.

## Group C — "Do nothing" options

The most common competitor is not a vendor. It's the status quo.

1. **Voicemail.**
   - Free, but callers often don't leave messages.
   - Best-sourced figures (CallRail 2025, as compiled by OnCrew): **42% leave a voicemail and ~58% do not. 82% say they'll call a competitor if not answered, and 21% do it immediately.**
   - The often-repeated "80–85% don't leave voicemail" and "85% never call back" have no credible primary source. **Do not use them.** Source: https://oncrew.ai/resources/missed-call-statistics
2. **Owner answers at night.**
   - Free in dollars, but costs sleep, family time, and burnout.
   - Works until the owner wants to step back, take a vacation, or grow.
3. **Forward to a tech's cell (on-call rotation).**
   - Common and cheap.
   - Problems: techs miss calls while driving or sleeping, quality varies, techs are poor at intake and pricing, and on-call duty is a retention issue.
   - Often paid as on-call pay or a per-call stipend (amounts vary; unverified).
4. **Hire a CSR / dispatcher.**
   - **BLS, Customer Service Representatives:** median $21.53/hr, $44,770/yr (latest BLS Occupational Outlook figure; see Market Facts). Older May 2023 OES data showed a $19.08/hr median. Sources: https://www.bls.gov/ooh/office-and-administrative-support/customer-service-representatives.htm and https://www.bls.gov/oes/current/oes434051.htm
   - **BLS, Dispatchers except police/fire/ambulance (May 2023):** median $22.53/hr ($46,860/yr). 10th–90th percentile is $15.44–$36.46/hr. Mean in the **building equipment contractors** industry (the HVAC, plumbing, and electrical bucket) is $22.43/hr ($46,660/yr). Source: https://www.bls.gov/oes/2023/may/oes435032.htm
   - **ZipRecruiter, HVAC CSR (Sept 2026):** average $18.80/hr, typical range $15.38–$20.91. Postings run $14–$24/hr. Source: https://www.ziprecruiter.com/Jobs/Hvac-Customer-Service-Representative
   - Newer BLS data (May 2025) has been released, and an early read showed CSR median around $18.08/hr (unverified; our fetch of the PDF was unreliable).
   - **The after-hours math:** One full-time CSR costs about $37k–$47k/yr in wages before payroll taxes and benefits. That covers about 40 hours a week, not nights or weekends. Nights plus weekends are about **128 hours a week**, so staffing them with people means multiple people or an answering service. That's why most small shops use an answering service or the owner's cell after hours.

## Where $497/mo for after-hours sits (honest positioning)

Be straight with yourself about this so you don't oversell.

- **Against cheap, generic AI tools (Rosie, Goodcall, Dialzara, Upfirst, Smith.ai AI):** We are **expensive**, often 2–10x their entry price. A price-only buyer who just wants "something picks up" can get that for $49–$150/mo. We only win here if we're clearly better at the contractor job: emergency triage, booking real jobs, and texting the on-call tech **[confirm in Product Truth]**. Don't compete on price with these.
- **Against contractor-specific AI (Sameday, Avoca):** We sit **in the middle**. We're about the same as Sameday Launch ($449/mo for 500 min) and likely well below Avoca's estimated range (unverified). Sameday and Avoca have certified or native FSM integrations and large funding. Do not claim integration parity unless Product Truth confirms it.
- **Against FSM-native AI (ServiceTitan, Housecall Pro, Jobber):** For a Jobber shop, Jobber's add-on is likely much cheaper. For ServiceTitan and Housecall Pro, the price isn't public, but it's native. Our angle is focus on after-hours and on-call handoff, plus a free pilot with nothing to buy first. **Do not claim we do something they can't unless Product Truth says so.**
- **Against human answering services:** We're **mid-range**.
  - $497 is more than the entry plans of most of these ($165–$350/mo).
  - But those entry plans cover only 50–250 minutes. A busy storm, freeze, or heat-wave night can blow through that, and overages run $1.30–$3.00/min or $8.50–$11.50/call.
  - If ReceptAI's $497 is flat and not metered **[confirm in Product Truth]**, the honest pitch is **predictable cost on the nights you get slammed.**
- **Against hiring a CSR:** We're **far cheaper** than staffing nights and weekends with people. Roughly $6k/yr compares with $37k–$47k+ per FTE in wages, and true after-hours coverage takes more than one FTE. But a daytime CSR does many things we don't. Position us as **covering the hours your CSR doesn't work**, not as replacing your CSR.
- **Against doing nothing:** The math is "one or two recovered jobs a month." Get the prospect's average ticket rather than assuming it. If one after-hours emergency call is worth $300–$1,500+ to them, $497 pays for itself with one or two booked jobs a month. Let them do the math with their own numbers. Don't invent ROI figures.

**Summary line for internal use:** We're a mid-priced, contractor-focused, after-hours specialist. We're not the cheapest robot and not the enterprise platform. The free 2-week pilot is our main risk-reducer.

## How to talk about competitors (rules for the agent)

1. **Never trash-talk.** Don't call any competitor bad, a scam, or overpriced. Don't repeat review complaints as facts about the prospect's vendor. Contractors are loyal to vendors that answer when it matters, and criticizing their choice criticizes them.
2. **Ask first, then listen.** Ask "What happens to a call at 9pm on a Saturday right now?" and "Who's answering after hours these days?" Let them name the competitor.
3. **Find the gap; don't create one.** Good gap questions:
   - "How often does the service actually get a job booked versus just taking a message?"
   - "When a no-heat call comes in at 2am, how does your on-call tech find out?"
   - "Any surprise bills during busy weeks?"
   - "How many of those after-hours messages turn into jobs?"
   - "Are your techs okay with the on-call phone?"
4. **If they're happy, respect it.** Say something like "Sounds like it's working. Want to run our pilot side by side on overflow for two weeks and compare?" Use that only if a side-by-side or overflow setup is possible **[confirm in Product Truth]**. Otherwise, ask permission to check back next season.
5. **Use only facts from this file, and say where they come from.** For example: "From what's published, a lot of answering services bill per minute with overages." Don't quote a competitor's exact price to the prospect unless they ask. If you do, say it's what's listed publicly and may have changed.
6. **Don't claim integration or capability parity you can't prove.** If the prospect runs ServiceTitan, Housecall Pro, or Jobber and asks how we compare to their built-in AI, give the honest answer from Product Truth. **[confirm in Product Truth]**
7. **Know where we lose.** We lose to:
   - Shops deep in ServiceTitan who want native, certified booking.
   - Very small shops who just want a $49 robot.
   - Owners who will only accept a live human.
   Qualify these out politely rather than forcing a deal.
8. **Lead with the pilot, not the comparison.** "Don't take my word for it. Run it on your after-hours line for two weeks free and look at the calls."
