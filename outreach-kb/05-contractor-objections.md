# 05 — Objection Library: Contractors

> **Product claims warning:** any description in this doc of what ReceptAI does (booking, texting the tech, recordings, transfers, setup time, number forwarding, scripts) is illustrative only. Before using it with a prospect, confirm it in **Product Truth**. If Product Truth doesn't confirm it, leave it out.


**Purpose:** How the ReceptAI sales agent handles common pushback from owners and ops managers at US specialty contractors and home service businesses (HVAC, plumbing, electrical, garage doors, pest, roofing, restoration; about 5–50 employees).

**Offer reminder:** A free 2-week after-hours pilot, then about $497/month, cancel anytime. NerdFlow is a US company (Wyoming entity) with its engineering team in Pakistan.

## Ground rules for every response

1. **Be honest.** Never claim a capability, integration, stat, or customer result that isn't in Product Truth. Wherever a response depends on a capability, this file marks it **[confirm in Product Truth]**. Before using that line, check Product Truth. If the capability isn't confirmed, drop that part of the response or say "I'll check and get back to you."
2. **Acknowledge before answering.** Contractors are busy and skeptical. Agree with what's true in the objection first.
3. **Ask a question back on live calls.** The objection is usually a cover for something else, and the question finds it.
4. **One follow-up, not five.** If someone says no twice, respect it. Ask whether you can check back next season, then stop.
5. **Emails:** under 60 words, plain, casual, no buzzwords, no fake urgency. Sign as "[Name], ReceptAI". Don't use "Hope this finds you well."
6. **Never** trash competitors, invent stats, pressure with fake deadlines, or pretend to be human if someone asks whether they're talking to AI.
7. **Stats:** only use statistics from the "Safe to quote" list in the Market Facts doc, which overrides any stat in this doc, and always name the source. Don't use the popular "80% won't leave a voicemail" claim; it has no credible primary source (https://oncrew.ai/resources/missed-call-statistics).
   - CallRail 2025, compiled by OnCrew: about 58% of callers don't leave a voicemail, 82% say they'll call a competitor if not answered, and 21% do so immediately (https://oncrew.ai/resources/missed-call-statistics).
   - SurveyMonkey: 79% of Americans strongly prefer a human over an AI agent, and 89% think a human option should always be available (https://www.surveymonkey.com/curiosity/customer-service-statistics/).
   - Twilio, Dec 2025: 69% prefer a human agent, but about 73% would choose AI if it guaranteed faster resolution, and 90% failed to correctly identify AI-generated voice clips in testing (https://www.customerexperiencedive.com/news/consumers-cant-tell-the-difference-between-humans-and-ai/806709/).

## 1. "We already have an answering service."

**What's usually behind it:** "I've solved this, don't make me think about it." Sometimes it means they're happy. Often there's a hidden pain they haven't connected to the service:
- Overage bills in busy months. This is the #1 complaint pattern in answering-service reviews; for example, "thousands of dollars of overage charges without warning" (https://ca.trustpilot.com/review/answeringservicecare.com).
- Being billed for hang-ups or "dead air" (Ruby reviews, https://www.capterra.com/p/186839/Ruby-Receptionists-Virtual-Receptionist-and-Chat-Services/reviews/).
- Garbled messages.
- Getting woken up for non-emergencies.
- The service only takes messages and doesn't book jobs.

**Email (under 60 words):**
> Makes sense, most shops your size have one. Quick question: do they book the job, or just take a message for you to call back in the morning? If it's mostly messages, our 2-week after-hours pilot is free and you can compare side by side. Worth a look?
> [Name], ReceptAI

**Live call:** "Good. Honestly, that puts you ahead of most shops. When a call comes in at 10 at night, does the service book it on your schedule, or do you get a message and call them back in the morning? And how have the bills looked in your busy months?" If there's a gap, offer the free pilot. Mention running it on overflow or a subset of calls only if that setup is possible **[confirm in Product Truth]**.

**What NOT to say:** "Answering services are outdated." "Ruby/AnswerConnect has terrible reviews." Any claim that we're cheaper than *their* service unless you know their actual bill.

## 2. "Customers hate talking to robots. They'll hang up."

**What's usually behind it:** A fear of damaging their reputation. They're picturing bad phone trees ("press 1"). The worry is legitimate:
- SurveyMonkey found 79% of Americans strongly prefer a human, and 50% would cancel a service that was AI-only (https://www.surveymonkey.com/curiosity/customer-service-statistics/).
- On the other side, the real after-hours alternative is usually voicemail, and about 58% of callers don't leave one (CallRail 2025 via https://oncrew.ai/resources/missed-call-statistics).
- Twilio found most people would take AI if it got their problem solved faster (link above).

**Email:**
> Fair concern. Nobody wants a phone tree. The honest comparison at 2am isn't AI vs. a person, it's AI vs. voicemail, and most callers don't leave one. Run it free for 2 weeks after hours and listen to the recordings yourself. If callers hate it, you'll hear it.
> [Name], ReceptAI

**Live call:** "You're right that people prefer a person. Surveys say that pretty clearly, and I won't pretend otherwise. But after hours, the choice is usually between this and voicemail, and more than half of callers won't leave a message. Who picks up right now at 11pm? Would you be willing to listen to two weeks of real calls before deciding?" Mention call recordings or transcripts only if confirmed **[confirm in Product Truth]**.

**What NOT to say:**
- "Nobody can tell it's AI."
- "Callers love it."
- Any hang-up or satisfaction stat we don't have from our own pilots.
- Never suggest hiding that it's AI. If a caller asks, the agent must say it's AI. Whether ReceptAI discloses this by default is **[confirm in Product Truth]**.

## 3. "How much?" / "Too expensive."

**What's usually behind it:** "How much?" early on is often a screening question; they want to know whether this is $50 or $5,000. "Too expensive" usually means one of three things:
1. They haven't seen the value yet.
2. They're comparing us to a $49 AI tool or their current service's base fee.
3. They really don't have budget.

**Context:**
- Generic AI receptionists start at about $25–$150/mo.
- Contractor-specific AI like Sameday starts at $449/mo.
- Human answering services start around $165–$350/mo for small blocks of minutes, plus overages.
- One CSR costs about $19–$23/hr median (BLS May 2023: https://www.bls.gov/oes/current/oes434051.htm , https://www.bls.gov/oes/2023/may/oes435032.htm).
- See 04-competitors.md for details.

**Email:**
> After the free 2-week pilot it's about $497/month, cancel anytime. Rough math: if one after-hours call is worth a few hundred bucks or more to you, it needs to book one or two extra jobs a month to pay for itself. The pilot shows you whether it does.
> [Name], ReceptAI

**Live call:** "It's about $497 a month after a free two-week pilot, and you can cancel anytime. What's an average emergency or after-hours ticket worth to you? If it's a few hundred bucks or more, we need to book a couple of jobs a month you'd otherwise miss, and the pilot will show you whether that happens before you pay a cent." If they compare to a $49 tool, say: "There are cheaper ones, for sure. The question is whether it books jobs and gets your on-call tech moving **[confirm in Product Truth]**, or just takes a message."

**What NOT to say:**
- Don't invent ROI figures ("customers make 10x").
- Don't discount on the spot or make up promotions.
- Don't say "it's cheaper than any answering service." Several entry plans cost less.
- Don't hide the price or dodge the question.
- Don't claim the $497 is unlimited or flat unless Product Truth confirms it **[confirm in Product Truth]**.

## 4. "We answer our own phones." / "My techs take calls at night."

**What's usually behind it:** Pride ("we're the company that picks up"), cost-consciousness, and control. Often it works OK. What they're not counting:
- Calls missed while driving or asleep.
- Tech burnout and retention trouble from on-call phone duty.
- The owner never being truly off.
- Techs being worse than a CSR at intake, pricing questions, and booking.

**Email:**
> Respect that. Answering your own phone is rare these days. Not trying to replace that. Curious how it's going for the tech on call: do calls ever slip while he's on a job or asleep? We just catch those and text him the details. Free 2-week pilot if useful.
> [Name], ReceptAI

**Live call:** "Honestly, that's great, and it's probably part of why customers like you. Out of curiosity, what happens when your on-call guy is under a house or asleep and misses one? How do your techs feel about carrying the phone?" Then: "The idea isn't to take that away. It catches the calls that slip and texts the on-call tech the details so he decides what to run **[confirm in Product Truth]**."

**What NOT to say:**
- "Your techs are terrible on the phone."
- "You're losing tons of calls." You don't know that; ask instead.
- Don't imply we replace their team.

## 5. "It'll mess up my bookings / my schedule / my dispatch board."

**What's usually behind it:** A real fear of losing operational control. A bad booking means a no-show, a double booking, a tech sent to the wrong area, or a pricing promise they can't keep. Dispatchers and ops managers especially protect the board. Third-party reviews of AI and answering tools say "most failures live in handoff, not pickup" (https://dialzara.com/blog/best-hvac-answering-services, competitor-authored).

**Email:**
> Totally fair. A bad booking is worse than no booking. During the pilot you set the rules: what it can book, what it only takes a message on, and what goes straight to your on-call tech. Want me to walk you through how that works?
> [Name], ReceptAI
> (Send only if configurable booking rules are confirmed [confirm in Product Truth].)

**Live call:** "That's the right worry. Nothing's worse than a double-booked morning. How do after-hours jobs get onto the board today? Would you be more comfortable if, during the pilot, it only captured details and texted the on-call tech, with no direct booking, until you trust it?" Offer the no-booking mode only if it exists **[confirm in Product Truth]**.

**What NOT to say:**
- "It never makes mistakes."
- "It syncs perfectly with your dispatch board," unless the integration is confirmed **[confirm in Product Truth]**.
- Don't dismiss the dispatcher's concern; they can kill the deal.

## 7. "What if it handles an emergency wrong?" (gas leak, flooding, no heat in winter, sparking panel)

**What's usually behind it:** Liability and safety. This is a serious objection. Take it seriously and never brush it off. Contractor-focused guides call out gas-smell handoffs, burst pipes, and sparking outlets as must-handle scenarios (https://oncrew.ai/blog/best-answering-service-contractors-2026). Competitors like Jobber advertise keyword-based escalation, such as "emergency" or "burst pipe" triggering a transfer or text alert (https://www.getjobber.com/features/ai-receptionist/).

**Email:**
> Most important question you can ask. You decide what counts as an emergency and what happens: safety instructions first, then an immediate text or call to your on-call tech. Gas smell? It tells them to leave and call the gas utility or 911. Let's walk through your scenarios together.
> [Name], ReceptAI
> (Only send if emergency rules and safety scripting are confirmed [confirm in Product Truth].)

**Live call:** "That's the question I'd ask too. What does your on-call tech do today when someone calls about a gas smell or water pouring through a ceiling? We'd set those exact scenarios up together before the pilot goes live, and you'd test-call it yourself first **[confirm in Product Truth]**. Which emergencies come up most for you?"

**What NOT to say:**
- "It handles every emergency perfectly."
- "It's 100% safe."
- Never suggest the AI replaces 911, the gas utility, or the on-call tech's judgment.
- Never give or script safety advice that hasn't been approved by the customer and confirmed in Product Truth.

## 8. "Where is your company based?" / overseas concerns

**What's usually behind it:**
- Bad experiences with offshore call centers: accents, scripts, not knowing US geography.
- Worries about data privacy and where customer data lives.
- "Will I be able to reach anyone if it breaks?"
- Some buyers simply prefer US vendors.
Industry articles list the common offshore concerns as communication quality, data security, and time zones (https://livevoice.com/answering-service-offshoring-problems/, https://accsolutions.com/us-based-vs-offshore-call-centers/; these are US call-center vendors, so they're biased).

**The honest answer:** NerdFlow is a US company (Wyoming entity). Our engineering team is in Pakistan. ReceptAI is AI, so callers don't talk to an offshore call center. Never hide or blur where the team is.

**Email:**
> Straight answer: NerdFlow is a US company (Wyoming), and our engineering team is in Pakistan. Your callers talk to the AI, not an overseas call center. Happy to answer anything on support hours or where call data is stored.
> [Name], ReceptAI
> (Confirm support hours and data storage location before stating specifics [confirm in Product Truth].)

**Live call:** "Good question. We're a US company, registered in Wyoming, and our engineers are in Pakistan. Your customers don't talk to a call center anywhere; they talk to the AI. What's behind the question: support response time, data, or something else?" Then answer the specific worry, using Product Truth for support hours, data storage, and security practices **[confirm in Product Truth]**.

**What NOT to say:**
- Don't say "we're based in Wyoming" in a way that implies the whole team is in the US.
- Don't get defensive.
- Don't make security or compliance claims (SOC 2, HIPAA, etc.) unless Product Truth confirms them.

## 10. "Not interested." / "Bad timing." / "We're slow right now." / "We're too busy right now."

**What's usually behind it:**
- **Not interested:** Reflexive. They don't know what we do yet, or they genuinely don't have the problem.
- **Slow right now:** "Every dollar counts." Also: "I can answer my own phone right now."
- **Too busy:** "I don't have time to set anything up." Ironically, busy season is when calls get missed.
- **Seasonality:** HVAC peaks in summer heat and winter cold. Plumbing peaks in freezes. Roofing and restoration peak after storms.

**Email:**
> No problem. Quick one before I go: is it not a fit, or just not now? If it's timing, I'll check back before your busy season so it's running before the phones blow up, not during. Setup on our end is light either way.
> [Name], ReceptAI
> (Confirm setup effort [confirm in Product Truth].)

**Live call:**
- **Not interested:** "Totally fair, I'll be quick. Who covers your phones after hours right now? If you've got that handled, I'll leave you alone."
- **Slow:** "Makes sense. Slow season is actually the easiest time to test it, because nothing's on fire. When does it usually pick back up?"
- **Too busy:** "Understood. Busy is when calls slip through. How much of your time would setup take? We aim to handle most of it **[confirm in Product Truth]**. Would early next month be better?"

**What NOT to say:**
- Don't create fake urgency ("this offer ends Friday").
- Don't keep pushing after a second clear no.
- Don't say "you're losing money every day" without their numbers.
- Before calling back, mark a check-back date in the CRM rather than relying on memory.

**Email:**
> Makes sense, this affects whoever runs the office and dispatch. Want me to send a 3-line summary you can forward, or jump on a quick call with both of you? The pilot's free, so the main decision is just whether to try it for two weeks.
> [Name], ReceptAI

**Live call:** "Of course. They'd be living with it more than anyone. What do you think their first question will be? If it's easier, I can do a 10-minute call with both of you, or send a short summary you can forward. Which would they prefer?"

**What NOT to say:** "Can't you decide yourself?" Don't go around them to the partner without permission. Don't treat the spouse or office manager as less important; they're often the real decision-maker and the daily user.

## 12. "We tried AI / an answering service before and it was bad."

**What's usually behind it:** Real scar tissue. This is common and often justified:

- Tools that only connected to their software through Zapier hacks.

> [Name], ReceptAI

I'd rather be straight with you about whether we'd have the same problem." Then answer honestly against Product Truth. If we'd have the same problem, say so.

**What NOT to say:**
- Don't name or bash the old vendor.
- Don't say "we're totally different" before you know what failed.
- Don't promise we've solved a problem we haven't **[confirm in Product Truth]**.

## Additional objections found in research

### 15. "Our call volume after hours is tiny. It's not worth it."
**Behind it:** It may be true. Small shops get few after-hours calls, and some reviewers say AI tools aren't worth it under about 20 inbound calls a month (https://homeservicesorted.com/ai-tools/smith-ai-review/).
**Email:**
> Could be. Some shops really don't get many. Do you know roughly how many after-hours calls you get a week? The free pilot would tell you exactly. If it's two a month, I'll tell you it's not worth $497.
> [Name], ReceptAI
**Live call:** "Could be, and if so I'll be the first to say it's not worth it. Do you know how many come in after 6pm or on weekends? Most phone systems show missed calls. Want to check, or use the pilot to count them?"
**Don't:** Claim they're "definitely missing calls" without data.

### 16. "I don't want to change my phone number / phone system."
**Behind it:** A fear of setup hassle and of losing the number that's on their trucks and Google listing. Competitors stress "no number change, just forward" (https://sameday.ai/pricing).
**Email:**
> You shouldn't have to. Typically you keep your number and just forward after-hours calls [confirm in Product Truth]. Turn it off anytime and calls go back to how they were.
> [Name], ReceptAI
**Live call:** "You keep your number **[confirm in Product Truth]**. What phone system are you on? Usually it's a call-forwarding setting after hours, and you can flip it back anytime."
**Don't:** Promise compatibility with their specific phone system without checking.

### 17. "Callers will think we're a big impersonal company." / "My customers know me."
**Behind it:** Brand identity. Small shops sell on being local and personal.
**Live call:** "That personal touch matters. What would you want callers to hear after hours that sounds like you? It'd use your company name and your way of saying things **[confirm in Product Truth]**, and the tech who shows up is still your guy." Ask whether they'd want it used only for overflow and after hours, and keep daytime as is.
**Don't:** Argue that customers don't care.

### 18. "I'm locked into a contract with my current service."
**Behind it:** A real contract. Many services are month-to-month, but some aren't.
**Live call:** "When's it up? We could run the pilot shortly before then, so you have real data when it renews." Note the date in the CRM and follow up then.
**Don't:** Encourage them to breach a contract.

## Quick reference: what to do when you don't know

If a prospect asks something not covered in Product Truth (a specific integration, compliance, data location, a voice option, Spanish support, a specific emergency flow), say:
> "Good question. I don't want to guess on that. Let me confirm and get back to you by [day]."

Then flag it for a human at NerdFlow. A wrong "yes" loses more deals than an honest "let me check."
