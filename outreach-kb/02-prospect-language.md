# 02 — Prospect Language Library

> **Product claims warning:** any description in this doc of what ReceptAI does (booking, texting the tech, recordings, transfers, setup time, number forwarding, scripts) is illustrative only. Before using it with a prospect, confirm it in **Product Truth**. If Product Truth doesn't confirm it, leave it out.


**Product:** ReceptAI, an AI receptionist that answers after-hours and overflow calls, books the job, and texts the on-call tech.
**Audience for this file:** the AI sales agent writing outreach to owners, GMs, ops managers and office managers at US specialty contractors (HVAC, plumbing, electrical, garage door, pest control, roofing, restoration), roughly 5-50 employees.
**Offer:** free 2-week after-hours pilot, then about $497/month.

## How to read this file

- Quotes come from contractor forums (ContractorTalk, PlumbingZone, Mike Holt Forums), a homeowner forum (Garage Journal), Fine Homebuilding, trade press (ACHR News), contractor podcasts, CBS News, and job postings. Each has a source URL.
- Quotes are as close to verbatim as the source allowed. Spelling and grammar are left as written ("alot", "dont", "independant"). That's how these people write, so don't correct it in your head.
- A quote marked **(paraphrase)** is a summary, not the speaker's exact words.
- Many forum posts are 5-15 years old. The language hasn't changed much, but treat them as voice samples, not current statistics.
- Quotes were pulled through an automated page reader. Before you put any quote in a customer-facing asset, open the URL and check it word for word.
- Reddit, HVAC-Talk and ElectricianTalk were blocked or paywalled during research. There are no Reddit quotes here.

### Patterns to take from the quotes

- **The phone is a cell in a truck.** Small operators forward the office line to their cell and miss calls while driving, on a roof, or in a crawlspace.
- **"Live person" is the bar.** Homeowners with an emergency hang up on machines and keep dialing. Contractors know this ("go to the next on the list").
- **Answering services get blamed for knowing nothing.** The complaint isn't that the call got answered. It's that whoever answered "can't answer the questions," "know nothing about your business," and only took a message. A message isn't a booked job.
- **Callback promises are worn out.** "Too many companies promise to call back so the customer doesn't believe it anymore." Callers want "a scheduled time now."
- **After-hours work is premium and painful.** Owners price it at time-and-a-half or double time, rotate a "duty pager," and resent being woken for things that aren't emergencies.
- **AI gets curiosity mixed with fear.** Even early adopters say "we'll always keep human CSRs." Pitch ReceptAI as cover for the hours nobody's in the office, not as a replacement for staff.

### Words and phrases to avoid (~22)

These sound like a SaaS pitch deck. Contractors stop reading when they hit them.

- omnichannel
- customer experience platform / CX
- leverage (as a verb)
- synergy
- seamless / seamlessly
- revolutionize / transform / game-changer / disrupt
- cutting-edge / state-of-the-art / next-gen
- AI-powered solution / AI-driven (say "AI receptionist" once, then talk about calls and jobs)
- conversational AI / LLM / agentic / voice agent stack
- digital transformation
- workflow automation / streamline operations
- touchpoints / customer journey
- empower / unlock / supercharge
- robust / scalable / enterprise-grade
- "Hope this email finds you well"
- "I wanted to reach out"
- "quick question" as a subject line on its own (overused)
- "replace your staff" / "fire your CSR" (scares owners and offends office managers)
- "never miss a call again" as a headline (every competitor uses it; it reads as an ad)
- "inbound lead management" / "lead capture" (they say "calls" and "jobs")
- "stakeholders" / "decision-makers"
- unexplained acronyms (SLA, CSAT, NPS)

## How to use this (rules for the AI)

1. **Talk calls and jobs, not technology.** Open with the situation ("phone rings at 9:40pm, nobody picks up, they call the next plumber on Google") and finish with the result ("ReceptAI books it and texts your on-call tech"). Mention AI no more than once per message.
2. **Mirror the trade's emergency.** No-heat or no-cool for HVAC, burst pipe or water heater for plumbing, broken spring for garage doors, power out or breaker for electrical, roof leak during a storm for roofing, water in the basement for restoration, wasps or bed bugs for pest control. Generic "customer inquiries" reads as spam.
3. **Pitch it as after-hours cover that sits beside their people, never as a replacement.** "Your office goes home at 5. This picks up after." Office managers are often the gatekeeper and future user. Make them look good: "it handles the 2am calls so you don't."
4. **Beat the answering-service objection in their own words.** Their complaint is "takes a message, knows nothing about the business." So say ReceptAI books the job on the schedule and texts the on-call tech. A booked job is the difference.
5. **Use the callback-speed truth without lecturing.** Customers "go to the next on the list" and contractors already know it. Say it once, plainly: "After hours, whoever answers first usually gets the job."
6. **Keep the tone short, plain and a little blunt.** These are working owners reading on a phone in a truck. Write under 90 words, no buzzwords, no fake familiarity. A small, dry joke is fine ("your phone shouldn't be the reason you sleep in your jeans").
7. **Make the ask small and risk-free.** "Want to try it on nights and weekends for two weeks, free?" Don't open with a demo request for a busy owner. Only say "no contract" or "month to month" if the commercial terms really are that.
8. **Never put a quote from this file in front of a prospect as if a customer said it.** The quotes are for learning their voice. Quote in outreach only if you attribute it to the public source and have checked it word for word.
