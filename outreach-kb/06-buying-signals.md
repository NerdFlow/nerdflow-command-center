# 06 — Buying Signals and Lead Scoring

> **Product claims warning:** any description in this doc of what ReceptAI does (booking, texting the tech, recordings, transfers, setup time, number forwarding, scripts) is illustrative only. Before using it with a prospect, confirm it in **Product Truth**. If Product Truth doesn't confirm it, leave it out.


**Purpose:** Tell the lead-finding agent which public signals show that a US specialty contractor (HVAC, plumbing, electrical, garage door, pest, roofing, restoration; roughly 5–50 employees) needs after-hours or overflow call coverage **now**, how to find each signal, how to score the lead, and when to reach out.

**Product reminder:** ReceptAI answers after-hours and overflow calls, books jobs, and texts the on-call tech. Offer: free 2-week after-hours pilot, then about $497/month.

**Rules for using this file**
- Score only on evidence you actually found. Record the URL and date for every signal.
- Never invent a signal. If you only *suspect* one (e.g. "probably uses voicemail at night"), log it as `suspected`. It earns no points.
- Signals go stale. A job posting older than 90 days or a review older than 12 months counts at half value.
- Use the signal to write the opener. One specific, true observation beats three generic claims.
- Statistics used in outreach must come from the "Safe to quote" list in `07-market-facts.md`.

## 1. Signal catalogue

Each signal lists: **Why it matters**, **How to find it**, **Strength**, and **Outreach angle**.

### 1.1 Job posting for a phone-facing role (CSR, dispatcher, receptionist, call center, overnight/after-hours dispatcher)
- **Why it matters:** They have a phone-coverage gap today and are budgeting to fill it. A CSR or dispatcher costs about $21.53–$24.20/hour median in wages alone (BLS 2025 medians; see `07-market-facts.md`), before benefits, training and turnover. An "overnight", "after-hours", "weekend" or "on-call answering" posting is the strongest version: it is exactly the gap ReceptAI fills.
- **How to find it:**
  - Indeed: `"customer service representative" HVAC [city]`, `"dispatcher" plumbing [state]`, `"CSR" "ServiceTitan"`, `"after hours dispatcher" HVAC`, `"overnight dispatcher" plumbing`, `"call center" "home services" [city]`.
  - Google: `site:indeed.com "HVAC" "dispatcher" "[city]"`, `site:ziprecruiter.com "plumbing" "customer service representative"`, `"weekend" "answering phones" HVAC hiring [city]`.
  - Also check LinkedIn Jobs, Glassdoor, ZipRecruiter, Craigslist "admin/office" and the company's own `/careers` or `/join-our-team` page.
  - Read the posting text for: "answer inbound calls", "book appointments in ServiceTitan/Housecall Pro", "evenings and weekends", "rotating on-call".
- **Strength:** **Strong** (overnight/after-hours/weekend posting); **Medium-Strong** (daytime CSR/dispatcher).
- **Outreach angle:** "Saw you're hiring a [CSR/dispatcher]. While you hire, we can cover nights and overflow so no calls drop. The 2-week after-hours pilot is free and it doesn't replace whoever you hire."
  Present ReceptAI as cover alongside the hire, not a replacement. Owners resist being told to skip a hire.

### 1.2 The same phone role posted again and again (turnover)
- **Why it matters:** Re-posting the same CSR/dispatcher role 2+ times in 6 months points to turnover, and each vacancy leaves the phones uncovered. BLS projects about 289,500 openings a year for customer service reps, mostly from turnover, even though the occupation is declining (BLS OOH, 2025–35). This is a high-churn role.
- **How to find it:** Search the company name plus the role on Indeed and Google. Compare posting dates, and check "posted 30+ days ago" plus re-listings. Use Google with date filters: `"[Company]" "customer service representative"`, Tools → past year. Look for a "we're always hiring" banner on the careers page.
- **Strength:** **Strong** (2+ distinct postings within 6 months); Medium (one long-open posting, 60+ days).
- **Outreach angle:** "Looks like the dispatcher seat has turned over a couple of times this year. That's brutal in peak season. We keep the phones covered between hires, and the AI doesn't quit in July."

### 1.3 Reviews complaining about responsiveness
- **Why it matters:** This is public evidence that calls are being missed and that it already costs them reputation.
- **How to find it:**
  - Google Maps / Business Profile reviews: open the reviews, sort by "Lowest" and "Newest", and scan for phrases.
  - Yelp, BBB (complaints section), Angi, Nextdoor (where visible), Facebook recommendations.
  - Google queries: `"[Company]" "never called back"`, `"[Company]" "went to voicemail"`, `"[Company]" "couldn't reach"`, `"[Company]" "no one answered"`, `"[Company]" "left a message"`, `site:yelp.com "[Company]" "voicemail"`.
  - Phrases to match: never called back, no call back, went to voicemail, voicemail full, couldn't reach, couldn't get through, no answer, didn't answer, called three times, after hours, weekend, emergency and nobody, called someone else, hired another company.
- **Strength:** **Strong** (2+ such reviews in the last 12 months, or 1 mentioning a night/weekend emergency); Medium (1 older review).
- **Outreach angle:** Be tactful. Don't quote the review back word for word, and don't shame them. Example: "Noticed a couple of customers mention trouble getting through after hours. Super common when the team's in the field. Here's how we handle that..." Never say "your reviews are bad."

### 1.4 Advertises 24/7 or emergency service
- **Why it matters:** They have publicly promised round-the-clock availability, so every unanswered night call breaks that promise. Emergency jobs also carry premiums: Angi reports emergency plumbing costs 1.5–3x standard rates, and after-hours HVAC adds $40–$80/hr (see `07-market-facts.md`). Night calls are high-value.
- **How to find it:** Website hero/header ("24/7", "24 hour", "emergency service", "always open", "same-day"). Google Business Profile hours set to "Open 24 hours". LSA/Google Ads text. Queries: `"24/7 emergency" plumber [city]`, `"24 hour" HVAC repair [city]`, `"emergency water damage" [city]`.
- **Strength:** **Medium** alone. **Strong** when combined with 1.3 (complaints) or 1.10 (voicemail after hours).
- **Outreach angle:** "You advertise 24/7. Who picks up at 2am right now? If it's the owner's cell or a voicemail box, we can take that call, book it, and text your on-call tech."

### 1.5 New location, new service area, or expansion
- **Why it matters:** Call volume grows faster than office staff. New territories often get routed to an existing office or a cell phone.
- **How to find it:** Google News `"[Company]" "new location"` / `"now serving"` / `"grand opening"`. Website "Service Areas" pages added recently. A new Google Business Profile in a nearby city. Local business journals. Press releases. LinkedIn company posts. Chamber of commerce ribbon-cuttings.
- **Strength:** **Medium**.
- **Outreach angle:** "Congrats on opening in [city]. New territory usually means the phones get stretched before the office team does. Want overflow and after-hours covered from day one?"

### 1.6 Hiring technicians fast (growth)
- **Why it matters:** More techs means more capacity to sell. Missing calls wastes that capacity, and the office team usually lags behind tech hiring.
- **How to find it:** 3+ open tech roles at once (Indeed, company careers page). "Sign-on bonus" for techs. LinkedIn headcount growth. Facebook "we're hiring" posts.
- **Strength:** **Medium** (rises to Strong when paired with a CSR posting).
- **Outreach angle:** "You're adding techs. Make sure every call that should land on their schedule gets booked, including the ones at 7pm."

### 1.7 Uses ServiceTitan, Housecall Pro, Jobber, FieldEdge, Service Fusion, or Workiz
- **Why it matters:** These companies already pay for software and run a scheduling system ReceptAI can book into. That signals tech comfort, a software budget and some operational maturity. ServiceTitan data shows top performers book 62% of inbound lead calls versus 39% for everyone else (ServiceTitan, May 2026).
- **How to find it:**
  - Website: "Book Online" widgets (Housecall Pro, ServiceTitan Scheduling Pro, Jobber client hub). Check the page source for script domains such as `housecallpro.com`, `servicetitan`, `scheduling pro`, `getjobber.com`, `workiz`.
  - Job postings: "experience with ServiceTitan preferred".
  - Customer invoices or review replies sometimes mention the platform.
  - ServiceTitan/Housecall Pro case studies and customer pages: `site:servicetitan.com "[Company]"`.
- **Strength:** **Medium** (fit and budget signal, not a pain signal).
- **Outreach angle:** "Since you're on [ServiceTitan/HCP], calls we answer can land as booked jobs in your board. No double entry." **Only say this if ReceptAI actually integrates with that platform. Otherwise say "we text the details to your tech and office."**

### 1.8 Runs Google Local Services Ads (LSA)
- **Why it matters:** This is a very strong economic angle.
  - LSA bills per lead, mostly phone calls. Typical cost per lead runs from about $39–$80 for electrical, HVAC and plumbing, about $71–$162 for roofing, and about $215 for water damage (agency benchmarks, vendor data; see `07-market-facts.md`).
  - **New since October 1, 2026:** Google bills missed calls during business hours as leads if the caller stays on the line more than 20 seconds. Follow-up calls can also become chargeable (Search Engine Roundtable, Aug 25 2026; PPC Land). A missed LSA call is now often **paid for and lost**.
  - Google's own LSA help page says: "If you regularly fail to answer calls or respond to messages, your ad ranking may be affected" (support.google.com/localservices/answer/6224841). Poor answering costs them both money and visibility.
- **How to find it:** Google `[trade] near [city]` and look for the "Sponsored" LSA carousel with the "Google Guaranteed"/"Google Screened" badge. Note which companies appear. Repeat across nearby zip codes and at different times of day, because an LSA ad shows only when the business has it running.
- **Strength:** **Strong** for fit (they pay per call). Combine with 1.3 or 1.10 for urgency.
- **Outreach angle:** "Saw you on Google's Local Services ads. Since Oct 1, Google charges for missed calls during business hours, and its own help docs say unanswered calls can hurt your ad ranking. We can answer those calls [confirm in Product Truth], including overflow when the office is slammed." Do not claim a specific dollar loss for *them*. You don't know their numbers.

### 1.9 Seasonal or weather-driven surge (now or imminent)
- **Why it matters:** Demand spikes overwhelm small office teams, and after-hours calls rise with them. ServiceTitan data: 14.1% of residential HVAC inbound calls arrived outside business hours in June 2025 versus 9.8% in October, with evening calls concentrated from 5–9pm (ServiceTitan, June 2026).
- **How to find it (by trade):**
  - **HVAC:** NWS heat advisories/excessive heat warnings, and cold snaps/hard freeze warnings (weather.gov). Search `heat advisory [city]` and `freeze warning [city]`.
  - **Plumbing:** hard freeze/"pipe burst" news, e.g. `"burst pipes" [city] [month]`.
  - **Roofing/restoration:** hail, wind and named storms (NWS Storm Prediction Center storm reports, NOAA NHC for hurricanes), flood warnings, `hail [city] [date]`.
  - **Pest:** spring/summer swarming season (termites, mosquitoes), local news on infestations.
  - **Garage door:** cold weather (broken springs are commonly reported in cold weather; *unverified* as a measured statistic).
- **Strength:** **Medium** (timing amplifier; it adds urgency to other signals rather than standing alone).
- **Outreach angle:** "With the [heat wave / freeze / hail] hitting [city] this week, your phones are probably ringing off the hook. We can switch on overflow and night coverage within [X] days, free for two weeks." **Only claim a setup time that ReceptAI actually supports.**

### 1.10 After-hours line goes to voicemail (verify ethically)
- **Why it matters:** This is direct evidence of the exact gap. CallRail's 2025 survey found 78% of consumers have abandoned a business after an unanswered call (vendor survey; see `07-market-facts.md`).
- **How to find it (allowed methods only):**
  - Website text: "leave a message and we'll call you back", "office hours M–F 8–5", "for emergencies call [cell number]", "calls after 5pm returned next business day", or an "after-hours: leave a voicemail" notice.
  - Google Business Profile hours that close at 5pm or are closed weekends, while the site claims emergency service. This mismatch is a good signal.
  - Contact page with only a form and "we respond within 1 business day".
  - Reviews that describe reaching voicemail (see 1.3).
- **Rules (MUST follow):**
  - **Do not** place fake "customer" calls, pose as a homeowner, or waste their on-call tech's time to test the line. That is deceptive and burns goodwill.
  - **Do not** use autodialers or robocalls to test numbers.
  - If a human on the sales team chooses to call, they identify themselves honestly ("Hi, this is [name] from ReceptAI..."). Hearing a voicemail greeting during an honest sales call at 6pm is fine to note.
  - Record what you observed and where, e.g. "website contact page says after-hours calls are returned next business day (URL, date)".
- **Strength:** **Strong** when documented on their site or reviews; Weak if only inferred from business hours.
- **Outreach angle:** "Your site says after-hours callers should leave a message. Most people won't, they just call the next company. We pick up instead, book the job, and text your on-call tech."

### 1.11 Owner is the answering service
- **Why it matters:** The owner's cell is listed as the emergency number, or reviews thank "[owner] for picking up at 11pm". This signals burnout and lack of coverage, which makes a great quality-of-life angle.
- **How to find it:** "For emergencies call [name] at [mobile]" on the site, a personal cell on the GBP, review text, Facebook posts.
- **Strength:** **Medium-Strong**.
- **Outreach angle:** "Looks like you're the after-hours line yourself. We take the 2am calls, book the real emergencies, and only wake your tech when it's real."

### 1.12 Recently acquired by a PE-backed platform
- **Why it matters (often negative):** HVAC M&A remains active. Capstone Partners counted 92 HVAC services transactions YTD 2026, with PE add-ons at 41.3% of deals (Capstone Partners, July 2026). Platforms often centralize call handling into a shared call center or switch to group-wide software, so the local GM may no longer own the phone decision.
- **How to find it:** Website footer "a [Platform] company", press releases `"[Company]" "acquired by"`/`"joins"`/`"partners with"`, and the platform's "our brands" page.
- **Strength:** **Negative to neutral.** Treat it as a likely poor fit unless evidence shows the location still runs its own phones (e.g. it's still hiring its own CSR).
- **Outreach angle (if pursued):** Target the platform's ops/contact-center lead with an overflow/night-shift pitch, not the local branch. Lower priority.

### 1.13 Weak or supporting signals (small points only)
- **Contact-form-only websites** or a "Text us" button with no phone answering promise. *Weak.*
- **Slow or no replies to Google reviews**, which suggest a stretched office. *Weak.*
- **Low review count with a high rating** suggests a small but growing business. *Weak (fit).*
- **Owner posting in trade Facebook groups or on Reddit** (r/HVAC, r/Plumbing) about missed calls or answering services. *Strong if found, but rare.* Don't scrape private groups, and don't quote personal posts.
- **Running Google Search Ads / Angi / Thumbtack:** they pay for calls, so the logic is similar to LSA. *Medium-Weak.*

## 2. Lead-scoring model (0–100)

Apply the steps in order. First check disqualifiers, then add points, then apply decay and caps.

### 2.1 Hard disqualifiers (stop, do not contact)
- Already uses an AI receptionist or AI voice agent. Look for: "Our AI assistant will answer", an AI disclosure in the greeting heard on an honest call, or a vendor case study/press release naming them (e.g. ServiceTitan AI virtual agent, Avoca, Sameday, similar).
- Franchise location whose brand runs a central national call center (the site says "call our national scheduling center", or there is one 1-800 number for all locations).
- More than 100 employees (likely an in-house call center and an enterprise buying process).
- Solo operator / one-person handyman with no employees (low call volume; $497/mo is unlikely to pay back).
- Not in the US.
- Out-of-scope business (e.g. a supply house, manufacturer or general contractor doing only new construction bids with no service calls).
- Previously opted out or asked not to be contacted.

### 2.2 Soft disqualifiers (cap score at 40)
- 51–100 employees (possible fit, but they probably have dispatch staff; treat as an overflow-only pitch).
- Acquired by a PE platform in the last 24 months (see 1.12).
- Already uses a live human answering service (e.g. the site mentions "our answering service"). They're a possible switch candidate, but the pitch is different.
- Commercial-only contractor with contracted service-level agreements (often has its own 24/7 dispatch).

### 2.3 Points

| # | Signal | Points |
|---|---|---|
| A | After-hours / overnight / weekend CSR or dispatcher job posting (≤90 days) | 25 |
| B | Daytime CSR / dispatcher / receptionist job posting (≤90 days) | 15 |
| C | Same phone role posted 2+ times in 6 months | +10 (on top of A/B) |
| D | 2+ reviews (≤12 months) mentioning voicemail / no callback / couldn't reach | 20 |
| D2 | Exactly 1 such review (≤12 months) | 10 |
| E | Documented after-hours voicemail / "leave a message" / next-business-day callback | 15 |
| F | Advertises 24/7 / emergency service | 8 |
| G | Running Google LSA (seen in the carousel) | 12 |
| H | Owner's cell is the emergency line | 8 |
| I | Uses ServiceTitan / Housecall Pro / Jobber / similar | 5 |
| J | 3+ open tech roles or a recent expansion / new location | 7 |
| K | Weather surge active or forecast in their area within 14 days | 5 |
| L | Employee count 5–50 (confirmed via LinkedIn / site / job posts) | 10 |
| M | Trade is plumbing, HVAC, restoration, garage door or electrical (emergency-heavy trades) | 5 |

- **Cap the total at 100.**
- **Decay:** postings older than 90 days and reviews older than 12 months score half.
- **Evidence rule:** every point needs a URL. No URL means no points.

### 2.4 Score bands and action

| Score | Band | Action |
|---|---|---|
| 70–100 | Hot | Contact within 24 hours. Use the strongest signal in the first line. Offer the free 2-week after-hours pilot directly. |
| 45–69 | Warm | Contact within the week. Lead with the relevant market fact plus the signal. |
| 25–44 | Nurture | Add to a seasonal sequence. Re-check signals before peak season for their trade. |
| 0–24 | Hold | Do not contact now. Re-scan in 90 days. |

### 2.5 Worked examples
- **Plumber, 18 employees, Dallas:** night dispatcher posting (25), 2 "went to voicemail" reviews (20), 24/7 advertised (8), LSA (12), size (10), trade (5), freeze warning (5) = **85, Hot.**
- **HVAC, 30 employees:** daytime CSR posting (15), on ServiceTitan (5), size (10), trade (5) = **35, Nurture.** Re-check in May before heat season.
- **HVAC, "a [Platform] company", 140 employees:** hard disqualifier (100+), **skip.**

## 3. Timing

**Caveat:** No independent study measures the best time to reach contractor owners specifically. The guidance below combines trade-specific operating data (ServiceTitan, Samsara) with general B2B calling heuristics that come from sales-tool vendors. Treat the B2B timing claims as **(unverified)** heuristics and A/B test them.

### 3.1 Time of day / day of week (general rules)
- **Owners/GMs:** try early, from 7:00–8:30am local, before techs roll and the office fills up, or late afternoon from 4:00–6:00pm after dispatch settles. Avoid 8–10am. CallRail data cited by a compiler names 8–10am and 5–7pm as home-services inbound call peaks (via aiclientbuilder.com; *unverified*). Those are their busiest phone windows.
- **Ops managers / office managers:** mid-morning (9:30–11:30am) Tuesday–Thursday.
- **Days:** prefer Tuesday–Thursday. Avoid Monday mornings (weekend backlog) and Friday afternoons. Sales-tool vendors (HubSpot, Salesloft, InsideSales via salesmotion.io) report higher connect rates mid-week and lower on Monday and Friday *(unverified; secondhand vendor claims)*.
- **Email:** send so it arrives around 6:00–7:30am local. Owners often triage on their phones before the day starts *(heuristic, unverified)*.
- **Never** cold-call during a declared local weather emergency in their area. Send a short email offering help instead.

### 3.2 Seasons by trade
Pitch **before** the peak, so they can set up and test during the 2-week pilot, and again **during** the peak when the pain is felt.

| Trade | Peak pain | Best outreach windows | Notes / sources |
|---|---|---|---|
| HVAC | Summer heat waves (June–Aug) and first cold snaps (Oct–Jan) | Apr–May (pre-cooling); Sept (the shoulder dip); during heat advisories | Samsara (65M HVAC trips, Sept 2023–June 2025): **October** is the busiest month, **September** the slowest, February the lowest. ServiceTitan: after-hours share peaks in June (14.1%). September is a good month to pitch because they have time to talk, and October/winter is coming. |
| Plumbing | Hard freezes (Dec–Feb), holidays (Thanksgiving/Christmas drain calls) | Oct–Nov (pre-freeze); during freeze warnings | ServiceTitan 2022: plumbing booking rates stable year-round ("when you need a plumber, you need them now"). Emergency demand is constant, so plumbing is a good year-round target. |
| Electrical | Storms/outages; summer load | Year-round; before storm season in your region | Less seasonal. Lead with overflow + LSA. |
| Garage door | Cold weather spring failures (*unverified*), year-round "car stuck" emergencies | Sept–Nov | Emergency nature ("can't get the car out") makes after-hours valuable. |
| Pest control | Spring–summer (Mar–Aug), termite swarms in spring | Jan–Mar (pre-season) | Fewer true night emergencies. Pitch overflow and weekend booking more than 2am coverage. |
| Roofing | Hail/wind/hurricane season (spring hail in Plains; June–Nov Atlantic hurricane season) | Feb–Apr (pre-hail); immediately after a storm hits *nearby* (not their own area while they're overwhelmed, unless offering instant help) | Storm-chasing surges are huge. Overflow coverage is the angle. |
| Restoration (water/fire/mold) | Freezes, storms, floods; true 24/7 | Year-round; after any freeze/flood event | 24/7 is core to the business. Angi average job is about $3,868, so every missed night call is very costly. |

### 3.3 Cadence
- Hot leads: Touch 1 on day 0 (email + LinkedIn view/connect). Touch 2 on day 2 (call, honestly identified). Touch 3 on day 5 (email with one stat + pilot offer). Touch 4 on day 10 (breakup email).
- Re-score and re-contact when a new signal appears (new posting, new review, weather event), referencing the new signal.
